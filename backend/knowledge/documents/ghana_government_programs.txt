Ghana Government Agricultural Programs and Policies

== PLANTING FOR FOOD AND JOBS (PFJ) PHASE I (2017-2022) ==
Launched April 2017 by President Akufo-Addo. Flagship agricultural transformation program.

Key features:
- 50% subsidy on improved seeds and fertilizers
- Target: 1.5 million farmers annually
- Goal: Increase yields of staple crops (maize, rice, soybean, sorghum)
- Budget: GH¢614 million (2022), GH¢660 million (2023)
- Achievements: Increased ag GDP growth rate, higher crop yields
- Limitations: Budget strain on government, limited value chain adoption, low access to credit

Five modules:
1. Food Crops (main PFJ)
2. Planting for Export and Rural Development (PERD)
3. Greenhouse Technology Villages (3 villages)
4. Rearing for Food and Jobs (RFJ) - livestock
5. Agricultural Mechanization Services Centers (AMSECs)

== PLANTING FOR FOOD AND JOBS PHASE II (PFJ 2.0) - 2023 to present ==
Launched August 28, 2023 at University for Development Studies, Tamale.
Five-year master plan (2023-2028).

Key changes from Phase I:
- Shift from direct input subsidy to smart agricultural input credit system (ICS)
- Linked to structured market arrangements (off-taker agreements)
- Private sector focus
- Digitized platform for management, monitoring, coordination
- Farmer registration with unique ID and biometrics
- Farm GPS mapping for size and commodity tracking

Eleven priority commodities:
- Grains: Maize, rice, soybean, sorghum
- Starchy staples: Cassava, yam, plantain
- Vegetables: Tomato, pepper, onion
- Poultry

Key actors in PFJ 2.0:
1. Input suppliers: Provide seeds, fertilizers, pesticides on deferred credit
2. Aggregators: Contract with farmers, supply inputs, arrange off-take
3. Farmers: Registered with biometrics, mapped farms
4. Service providers: AMSECs (mechanization) and Extension Agents
5. Warehouse Infrastructure & Collateral Managers (WICMs): Storage
6. Off-takers: Industry, processors, NAFCO, market women
7. Financial institutions: Insurance, GIRSAL (guarantee cover)

== CODAPEC / HI-TECH PROGRAM ==
Managed by COCOBOD since 2001/2002 season.
Free nationwide cocoa pest/disease control and fertilizer program.

Components:
1. Mass Spraying: Capsids/insects (April-May, August-September) - 1.4 million ha covered
2. Black Pod Control: Fungicide application - 1.0 million ha covered
3. Hi-TECH Fertilizer: Granular and liquid fertilizers, soil amendments

Structure:
- National Coordinator + 3 Deputy Coordinators (Insect Control, Black Pod, Fertilizer)
- 7 Regional Coordinators, 70 District Coordinators
- Over 60,000 trained sprayers nationwide
- Free to all mapped and productive cocoa farms

2024-2025 challenges: COCOBOD GHS 33 billion debt burden affected input procurement.
Black pod outbreaks in 72 districts due to excessive rainfall.
Production fell to ~590,000 metric tons (target was 610,000).

== REARING FOR FOOD AND JOBS (RFJ) ==
Livestock module under PFJ umbrella.
Focus: Poultry, small ruminants, pigs.
Poultry Intensification Scheme (PIS) supports poultry farmers.

== GHANA TREE CROP DIVERSIFICATION PROJECT ==
- World Bank IDA: $200 million (2023)
- Managed by COCOBOD and Tree Crops Development Authority
- Goal: Modernize and diversify tree crop production
- Crops: Cocoa plus cashew, coconut, rubber

== AGRI-NET (GHANA AGRICULTURAL SECTOR INVESTMENT PROGRAM) ==
World Bank-supported program.
Recent: Ghana launched AgriConnect Compact (June 3, 2026).
- National framework to strengthen food security, create jobs, reduce imports
- Priority value chains: Cocoa, oil palm, rice, maize, poultry
- Also: Cashew, coconut, rubber, fisheries, forest economy
- First phase: 2026-2030
- Backed by World Bank Group, AfDB, IFAD, Google, Bayer

== GHANA CARES (GHANA COVID-19 ALLEVIATION AND REVITALIZATION OF ENTERPRISES SUPPORT) ==
Includes agricultural components:
- Interest rate subsidy for agribusinesses
- Support for commercial farming
- Incentives for import substitution

== FERTILIZER POLICY ==
- 2008: Voucher-based 50% subsidy reintroduced
- 2017-2022: PFJ Phase I - 50% fertilizer subsidy
- 2023: Suspended during PFJ review
- PFJ 2.0: Input credit system (no direct subsidy)
- New fertilizer blends (CSIR-SRI validated): Zone-specific NPK blends for maize, cassava, soybean, rice
- AGRA and MoFA launched Fertilizer Bulk Blending Guidelines (April 2024)
- Previous blanket recommendation: 2 bags 15-15-15 + 1 bag SoA per acre

== COCOA PRICING AND REGULATION ==
- Producer price is regulated by COCOBOD annually
- 2024/25 price: GH¢48,000 per tonne (increased from GH¢20,928)
- 2025/26: GH¢65,000 per tonne (announced September 2025)
- Ghana is 2nd largest cocoa producer globally (after Cote d'Ivoire)
- All cocoa exports handled through COCOBOD

== TRADE POLICIES ==
- August 2024: Temporary ban on grain exports (corn, rice) during drought
- Benchmark Value Discount: Withdrawn in 2023 budget
  - Local producers supported, importers opposed
  - Resulted in higher imported food prices
- Ghana does not produce wheat; all wheat is imported (850,000 MT annually)
- Major export crops: Cocoa, gold, oil, cashew, timber

== EXTENSION SERVICES ==
- Extension agent to farmer ratio: ~1:1,500 (below FAO recommendation of 1:500)
- Under PFJ 2.0: Extension agents assigned to aggregators in production zones
- Digital tools: Cropping Calendar and Agro-Advisory System (CSIR-INSTI, CSIR-CRI, CGIAR)
- GMet and MoFA collaboration: Customized district-specific production calendars (FSRP)

== CLIMATE ADAPTATION PROGRAMS ==
- West Africa Food System Resilience Programme (FSRP): 20 automated weather stations installed
- Cropping Calendar and Agro-Advisory System: Real-time weather + crop phenology + soil data
- Ghana Climate Atlas (GMet): Climate projections for agricultural planning
- Ministry of Food and Agriculture (MoFA) leads climate-smart agriculture initiatives
