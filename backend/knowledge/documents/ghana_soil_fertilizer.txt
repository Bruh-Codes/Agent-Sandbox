Ghana Soil Types and Fertilizer Recommendations

== GHANA SOIL CLASSIFICATION ==
Ghana's soils have been extensively mapped by the CSIR-Soil Research Institute (CSIR-SRI). The major soil types by agro-ecological zone:

=== Forest Zone (Western, Western North, Ashanti, Eastern, Central) ===
- Dominant soils: Ochrosols and Oxysols (Forest Ochrosols, Rubrisols)
- Characteristics: Well-drained, moderate to high organic matter (2-4%), reddish-brown
- pH range: 4.5-5.8 (acidic) - recent 2025 digital soil mapping confirms pH <5.5 in many forest areas
- Limitations: Low available phosphorus, acidic conditions can limit productivity
- Organic carbon: 1.5-3.0% (relatively higher than other zones)

=== Forest-Savannah Transition Zone (Bono, Bono East, Ahafo, parts of Ashanti) ===
- Dominant soils: Forest-savanna mosaic soils, Lixisols
- Characteristics: Moderately fertile, sandy loams over clay subsoils
- pH range: 5.5-6.8
- Organic matter: 1.0-2.0%
- Limitations: Susceptible to compaction, moderate erosion risk

=== Guinea Savannah Zone (Northern, Savannah, North East, parts of Bono) ===
- Dominant soils: Plinthosols, Lixisols, Luvisols
- Characteristics: Sandy loams and loamy sands, low organic matter (<1.5%)
- pH range: 5.8-7.0 (less acidic than forest zone)
- Limitations: Low nitrogen, prone to erosion, low water-holding capacity
- Potassium levels: Generally adequate, with higher levels in upper northern areas

=== Sudan Savannah Zone (Upper East, Upper West) ===
- Dominant soils: Leptosols, Fluvisols, Vertisols
- Characteristics: Shallow soils on rock outcrops, sandy
- pH range: 6.0-7.2
- Organic matter: Very low (<1%)
- Limitations: Very low fertility, drought-prone, erosion risk

=== Coastal Savannah Zone (Greater Accra, parts of Central and Volta) ===
- Dominant soils: Sandy loams and clays, Acrisols, Gleysols
- Characteristics: Low organic matter (<1%), well-drained
- pH range: 5.0-6.5
- Limitations: Low organic matter, salinity in low-lying areas

=== Volta Region (incl. Oti) ===
- Varied soils: Acrisols, Nitisols, Leptosols (hilly terrain)
- pH: 5.0-6.5
- Note: High rainfall leads to leaching, limiting fertility

== NATIONAL SOIL FERTILITY STATUS (2025 Digital Soil Mapping) ==
Key findings from the 2025 Geoderma study on Ghana's digital soil mapping:
- Nitrogen levels decrease progressively from southern to northern Ghana
- Most soils are nitrogen-deficient (below maize critical range of 400-1500 ppm)
- Phosphorus is below maize critical threshold of 11 ppm in most areas
- Potassium is generally adequate (within 17-74 ppm), exceeding 74 ppm in upper northern areas
- Ghanaian soils are predominantly sandy
- Forest and coastal savanna zones show pH below 5.5 (acidity)

== FERTILIZER RECOMMENDATIONS ==

The CSIR-Soil Research Institute (CSIR-SRI) conducted validation trials across 17 districts in Brong-Ahafo and 15 districts in Northern regions (1,650 farmers) to develop zone-specific recommendations.

=== Maize Fertilizer Recommendations ===

Forest-Savannah Transition Zone:
- NPK recommendation: 90-60-60 (N-P2O5-K2O) + 1.7 kg Zn per hectare
- Recommended blends: 8 bags/ha NPK 15-20-20 + 0.7% Zn + 2 bags/ha urea
- Equivalent: 6 bags/ha 15-20-20 blend + 2 bags/ha urea (per CSIR-SRI validation)

Guinea Savannah Zone:
- NPK recommendation: 100-40-40 (N-P2O5-K2O) per hectare
- Recommended blends: 4 bags/ha NPK 15-20-20 + 2 bags/ha urea
- Old blanket recommendation (still used): 2 bags 15-15-15 + 1 bag sulfate of ammonia per acre
- Split application: Apply all P and K at planting; apply N in 2 splits (50% at planting, 50% at 4-5 weeks)

=== Cassava Fertilizer Recommendations ===

Forest-Savannah Transition / Guinea Savannah:
- NPK recommendation: 100-30-30 (N-P2O5-K2O) per hectare
- Recommended blends: 6 bags/ha NPK 17-10-10 + 2 bags/ha urea OR 4.5 bags/ha sulfate of ammonia
- Apply 2-4 weeks after planting for best results
- Note: Cassava is relatively tolerant of low fertility but responds well to K application

=== Rice Fertilizer Recommendations ===

Lowland Rice (Guinea Savannah and Transition):
- NPK recommendation: 90-60-60 (N-P2O5-K2O) per hectare
- Recommended blends: 6 bags/ha NPK 15-20-20 + 2 bags/ha urea OR 4 bags/ha SoA

Upland Rice (Guinea Savannah and Transition):
- NPK recommendation: 90-40-40 (N-P2O5-K2O) per hectare
- Recommended blends: 4 bags/ha NPK 15-20-20 + 3 bags/ha urea OR 6 bags/ha SoA
- Split N application: 50% at planting, 50% at tillering (30-35 DAP)

=== Soybean Fertilizer Recommendations ===

All Ecologies:
- NPK recommendation: 20-60-30 + 0.8 kg Zn per hectare
- Recommended blends: 4 bags/ha NPK 12-30-17 + 0.4% Zn
- Note: Soybean fixes its own N through rhizobium; P is the most critical nutrient
- Rhizobium inoculant recommended at planting for improved nitrogen fixation

=== Cocoa Fertilizer ===
- Recommended: NPK 0-22-18 + 9CaO + 7MgO at 300 kg/ha annually
- Applied April-June (early rains) for main crop
- Under CODAPEC/Hi-TECH: NPK + Zinc applied April-June
- Cocoa requires relatively low N but high K and Mg due to pod production
- Annual nutrient removal by 1 ton of dry cocoa beans: approximately 20 kg N, 3 kg P, 25 kg K

=== Tomato and Vegetables ===
- NPK 15-15-15 at 200-300 kg/ha at transplanting
- Top-dress with urea or NPK 15-15-15 3-4 weeks after transplanting
- Supplemental calcium to prevent blossom-end rot

=== Groundnut ===
- Low N requirement (legume), but responds to P and K
- Recommended: 200 kg/ha single superphosphate (SSP)
- Note: Avoid N fertilizers as they reduce nodulation

== INTEGRATED SOIL FERTILITY MANAGEMENT (ISFM) ==
MoFA and CSIR-SRI recommend ISFM approach combining:
1. Mineral fertilizers (site-specific blends)
2. Organic inputs (compost, manure at 5-10 t/ha)
3. Improved germplasm (high-yielding, nutrient-efficient varieties)
4. Legume-based rotations and intercropping
5. Minimum tillage and residue retention

== FERTILIZER SUBSIDY HISTORY ==
- 2008: Government introduced 50% fertilizer subsidy via voucher system
- 2017-2022: Under PFJ Phase I, seeds subsidized 50%, fertilizers 50%
- 2023: Subsidies suspended during PFJ review; transition to PFJ 2.0 input credit system
- PFJ 2.0 (2023-present): Shift from direct subsidy to smart input credit linked to market arrangements

== CHALLENGES ==
- Only ~20% of farmers use fertilizers (MoFA, 2017 baseline)
- Blanket recommendations still widely used despite zone-specific alternatives
- Lack of effective soil testing services for farmers
- High fertilizer costs due to import dependence and cedi depreciation
- Soil acidity increasing in forest zones due to continuous cropping without liming
