Ghana Pests and Diseases Control Guide

== COCOA PESTS AND DISEASES ==

=== Black Pod Disease (Phytophthora megakarya / P. palmivora) ===
- Status: Most destructive cocoa disease in Ghana
- Season: Worsens during heavy rains (June-September)
- 2025 outbreak: Reported across 72 cocoa-growing districts due to excessive rainfall
- Symptoms: Brown spots on pods spreading rapidly, internal brown rot
- Spread: Splashing rain, water currents, wind, ants
- Losses: Can reduce harvest by 30% or more
- Control under CODAPEC:
  - Mass spraying rounds: April-May (first round), August-September (second round)
  - Copper-based fungicides (e.g., Kocide, Ridomil) applied to trunk and branches
  - Weekly removal of infected pods combined with chemical application
  - ~1.0 million hectares covered for black pod control
- Integrated management: Pruning for air circulation, shade optimization, resistant varieties

=== Cocoa Swollen Shoot Virus (CSSVD) ===
- Status: Long-term threat, worsening in Ghana
- 2024-2025: Ravaged plantations for two consecutive seasons
- Symptoms: Stem swelling, vein clearing, leaf blotches/streaks
- Yield impact: 30-50% reduction
- Spread: Mealybugs (Planococcus spp.)
- Control: No cure available
  - "Cutting-out approach": Infected trees must be ripped out and burnt
  - COCOBOD secured $100 million from World Bank for rehabilitation
  - Shade reduces severity
  - Replanting with tolerant/resistant varieties after fallow period

=== Mirids / Capsids (Distantiella theobroma, Sahlbergella singularis) ===
- Status: Major insect pest of cocoa in Ghana
- Symptoms: Pod lesions, dieback of shoots, cankers on branches
- Control: CODAPEC mass spraying (April-May and August-September)
- Insecticides applied free to farmers by trained spraying gangs
- ~1.4 million hectares covered for insect control

=== Cocoa Pod Borer (Conopomorpha cramerella) ===
- Emerging threat in West Africa
- Larvae bore into pods, destroying beans
- Control: Sanitation (remove and destroy infested pods), regular harvesting

== MAIZE PESTS ==

=== Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) ===
- Status: Most serious maize pest in Ghana since 2016-2017 outbreak
- Host range: Over 353 plant species, primarily Poaceae, Asteraceae, Fabaceae
- Symptoms: "Windowing" on leaves, whorl damage, frass, stunted growth
- Yield loss: Up to 34% in severe infestations
- Monitoring: FAO's FAMEWS (Fall Armyworm Monitoring and Early Warning System)
- Integrated Pest Management (IPM) approach recommended:

Cultural control:
  - Early planting to avoid peak pest populations
  - Avoid staggered planting
  - Clean cultivation and weed management
  - Crop rotation
  - Balanced fertilizer application

Biological control:
  - Natural enemies: Cotesia ruficrus (parasitoid), spiders, predators
  - Egg parasitoids (Trichogramma spp.) being researched in Ghana
  - Neem-based botanicals
  - Lemon basil (Ocimum africanum) water extract: trials in Northern Region show efficacy
  - Entomopathogenic fungi and nematodes under development

Chemical control (when IPM insufficient):
  - Emamectin benzoate
  - Chlorantraniliprole
  - Lambda-cyhalothrin
  - Cartap hydrochloride
  - Apply when threshold of 20% infested plants is reached
  - Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides that kill beneficials

=== Stem Borers (Busseola fusca, Sesamia calamistis) ===
- Symptoms: "Dead heart" in young plants, stem tunneling, broken stalks
- Control: Crop rotation, destruction of crop residues, early planting

=== Larger Grain Borer (Prostephanus truncatus) ===
- Major post-harvest pest of maize and cassava
- Control: Proper drying, hermetic storage (GrainPro bags, silos), Ateco insecticide

== VEGETABLE PESTS ==

=== Tomato: ===
- Tuta absoluta (Tomato leafminer): Larvae mine leaves, bore into fruits
  - Control: Pheromone traps, selective insecticides (indoxacarb, chlorantraniliprole)
- Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum): Wilting, vascular discoloration
  - Control: Resistant varieties, crop rotation, soil solarization
- Late blight (Phytophthora infestans): Leaf lesions, fruit rot
  - Control: Mancozeb or copper-based fungicides, preventive application in wet season
- Early blight (Alternaria solani): Target spots on leaves
- Blossom-end rot: Calcium deficiency (physiological, not pathogen)
  - Control: Consistent watering, calcium application

=== Pepper: ===
- Aphids, thrips, whiteflies: Control with neem oil, imidacloprid
- Anthracnose (Colletotrichum spp.): Fruit rot
  - Control: Mancozeb + copper fungicide rotation

=== Onion: ===
- Thrips (Thrips tabaci): Silvering of leaves, stunted bulbs
  - Control: Spinetoram, lambda-cyhalothrin, crop rotation
- Purple blotch (Alternaria porri): Purplish leaf lesions
  - Control: Chlorothalonil or mancozeb

== CASSAVA PESTS AND DISEASES ==

=== Cassava Mosaic Virus (CMV/CMD) ===
- Symptoms: Mosaic pattern, leaf distortion, stunting
- Control: Resistant varieties (e.g., Afisiafi, Essam Bankye)

=== Cassava Green Mite (Mononychellus tanajoa) ===
- Symptoms: Leaf drying, defoliation
- Control: Resistant varieties, predatory mites

=== Cassava Mealybug (Phenacoccus manihoti) ===
- Control: Biological control (Anagyrus lopezi parasitoid), neem sprays

== YAM PESTS AND DISEASES ==

=== Yam Beetle (Heteroligus meles) ===
- Symptoms: Holes in tubers
- Control: Seed dressing with chlorpyrifos or imidacloprid

=== Yam Mosaic Virus ===
- Control: Use of virus-free seed yams

=== Anthracnose (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides) ===
- Control: Resistant varieties, mancozeb spray

== GENERAL PEST MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES FOR GHANA ==

1. Integrated Pest Management (IPM): Use cultural, biological, and chemical methods in combination
2. Monitoring and scouting: Regular field inspection is essential
3. Threshold-based action: Apply control measures only when pest populations reach economic threshold
4. Pesticide rotation: Avoid using same chemical class repeatedly to prevent resistance
5. Proper pesticide handling: Use PPE, follow label rates, observe pre-harvest intervals
6. Preservation of natural enemies: Avoid broad-spectrum pesticides when possible
7. Government support: Contact MoFA district offices for pest alerts and extension advice

=== CODAPEC PROGRAM (Cocoa Disease and Pest Control) ===
- Launched: 2001/2002 cocoa season
- Managed by: Ghana Cocoa Board (COCOBOD)
- Coverage: All mapped and productive cocoa farms
- Components:
  - Mass spraying (insects): April-May and August-September, ~1.4 million ha
  - Black pod control: Fungicide application, ~1.0 million ha
  - Fertilizer/Hi-TECH: Granular and liquid fertilizers
- Staff: Over 60,000 trained sprayers plus coordinators
- Cost: Free to farmers
